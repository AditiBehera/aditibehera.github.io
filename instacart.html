Case study: a cancellation spike at one retailer

All data is synthetic: 6 fictional retailers, 12 weeks, about 240,000 orders, built to mirror the cancellation, reschedule and timeliness problems the PEO team owns. The SQL is written for Snowflake.

2 · DetectThe obvious alert rule would have missed most of it

A common first rule flags a week when its rate sits 3 standard deviations above the trailing 6 weeks. Run on this data, it raised two alerts. One was false: Retailer A moved only 0.3 points, but its history is so steady that a tiny move looked significant. Worse, after Week 9 the spike entered Retailer C's own baseline, so Weeks 10–12 stopped looking unusual.

I changed two things. Flagged weeks are excluded from future baselines, and an alert also needs a move of at least 0.5 points so it matters operationally.

Retailer · WeekRateBaselineChangeSimple ruleRevised rule

Result: the simple rule raised 2 alerts, 1 of them false, and missed 3 of the 4 spike weeks. The revised rule caught all 4 spike weeks with no false alerts.

-- Weekly cancel rate per retailer, scored against a clean trailing baseline
WITH weekly AS (
  SELECT o.retailer_id,
         DATE_TRUNC('week', o.delivery_window_start)  AS wk,
         COUNT_IF(o.status = 'cancelled') / COUNT(*)   AS cancel_rate
  FROM   fulfillment.orders o
  GROUP  BY 1, 2
),
labeled AS (                                  -- weeks already confirmed as incidents
  SELECT w.*, (i.wk IS NOT NULL) AS is_incident
  FROM   weekly w
  LEFT JOIN ops.confirmed_incidents i
         ON i.retailer_id = w.retailer_id AND i.wk = w.wk
),
scored AS (                                   -- incident weeks are NULLed out of the baseline
  SELECT l.*,
         AVG(IFF(is_incident, NULL, cancel_rate))    OVER w6 AS base_mean,
         STDDEV(IFF(is_incident, NULL, cancel_rate)) OVER w6 AS base_sd
  FROM   labeled l
  WINDOW w6 AS (PARTITION BY retailer_id ORDER BY wk
                ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND 1 PRECEDING)
)
SELECT retailer_id, wk, cancel_rate, base_mean,
       (cancel_rate - base_mean) / NULLIF(base_sd, 0) AS z
FROM   scored
WHERE  (cancel_rate - base_mean) / NULLIF(base_sd, 0) >= 3
  AND  cancel_rate - base_mean >= 0.005;     -- and at least 0.5 pts

3 · DiagnoseOne cancellation reason explains almost all of it

Splitting Retailer C's cancellations by reason, four of the five reasons barely move. "Store closed before pickup" goes from 1.9 to 22.0 per 1,000 orders. Drilling into stores, 6 of Retailer C's 40 stores account for 81% of the extra cancellations. All six shortened their evening hours in Week 9, but the slot scheduler kept offering pickup windows based on the old hours.

-- Which stores drive the excess "store closed" cancellations?
SELECT o.store_id,
       COUNT_IF(o.cancel_reason = 'store_closed')                          AS closed_cancels,
       RATIO_TO_REPORT(COUNT_IF(o.cancel_reason = 'store_closed')) OVER () AS share_of_total,
       MAX(h.hours_changed_at)                                             AS last_hours_change,
       COUNT_IF(o.slot_end_local::TIME > h.close_time_local)               AS slots_after_close
FROM   fulfillment.orders o
JOIN   retailer.store_hours h ON h.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE  o.retailer_id = 'C'
  AND  o.delivery_window_start >= :week_9_start
GROUP  BY o.store_id
ORDER  BY closed_cancels DESC;

5 · RecommendThree fixes, each with an owner

  1. Stop selling slots after closing time. Ask Engineering to block any pickup window that ends after the store's current close time. This removes the defect at the source.
  2. Add a store-hours freshness check to the retailer quality dashboard. Flag any store whose hours changed in the last 14 days, so Retailer Ops can confirm the change reached scheduling.
  3. Ship the revised alert rule. Clean baselines plus a minimum change, so a persistent spike keeps alerting and steady retailers stop raising false alarms.

6 · ValidateChecks before anything goes in a deck

  • Order counts reconcile to the weekly totals, with no duplicate order IDs.
  • Weeks are bucketed in each store's local time zone, so late-evening orders don't land in the wrong week.
  • The "after" period excludes partial weeks.
  • When an AI assistant drafts SQL like the store-level query, I check its output by hand against a few stores before trusting it.
